# EotG Frontier Systems — Final Design

> **Status (2026-10-04):** human-authored design brief. This is the source of truth for intent. It is turned into a buildable spec at `docs/specs/frontier_v1.md` (Pipeline A: spec → lore review → build → QA). Do not edit this file to record implementation decisions. Record those in the spec.

## 1. Purpose

**Frontier Systems** is a modular CK3 feature for representing star systems that are not yet fully settled but are beginning to develop a permanent, organized presence.

The feature must be completely functional **without Greater Drifts** or any other future EotG system.

Its core gameplay loop is:

> **Unsettled → Frontier → Settled**

A Frontier is a temporary developmental state, not a replacement for normal CK3 political systems.

The system is intended for the 866 **After the Crucible** start and should remain useful throughout the galaxy's history.

---

## 2. Core Design Principles

### 2.1 Modular by Design

Frontier Systems must not depend on:

- Greater Drifts
- Patronage
- Corporate governments
- Specialized galactic organizations
- A custom faction system
- A custom claims system
- A custom adventurer progression system
- A custom government system

Future systems may interact with Frontiers through generic integration hooks.

### 2.2 Enhance CK3 Rather Than Replace It

Once a Frontier becomes established, normal CK3 systems should take over wherever practical.

Frontier Systems should create conditions for:

- Holdings
- Characters
- Dynasties
- Development
- Buildings
- Culture
- Religion
- Titles
- Governments

rather than creating parallel permanent versions of those systems.

### 2.3 Frontier Is Temporary

A Frontier exists because something is being established.

Once the settlement becomes sufficiently established, the Frontier state ends and the system becomes part of the normal political/economic game.

---

# 3. Frontier States

## 3.1 Unsettled

An **Unsettled** system has no recognized permanent settlement of sufficient importance to count as established.

Unsettled does **not** necessarily mean literally empty.

A system may contain:

- Hidden settlements
- Nomadic populations
- Ancient ruins
- Isolated communities
- Hostile inhabitants
- Undiscovered resources
- Other unknown features

The important distinction is that no permanent settlement has yet been established and recognized by the surrounding political order.

### Typical interactions

- Explore
- Discover
- Establish Frontier
- Investigate
- Scout
- Establish an expedition

---

## 3.2 Frontier

A **Frontier** is an Unsettled system where a permanent or organized settlement project has begun.

It represents the uncertain period where:

- Infrastructure is being established
- Population is arriving
- Resources are being exploited
- Political influence is forming
- The settlement may succeed or fail

A Frontier is therefore a **developmental state**, not a government.

---

## 3.3 Settled

A system becomes **Settled** when its Frontier Project reaches the requirements for permanent establishment.

At this point:

- The Frontier state ends.
- Its development becomes part of normal CK3 gameplay.
- Normal titles, holdings, governments, characters, buildings, cultures, and religions can take over.

A successful Frontier should not remain trapped in a permanent special mechanic.

---

## 3.4 Abandoned

A Frontier Project can fail or be abandoned.

An abandoned system should normally become available for future settlement rather than being treated as if its history never existed.

Possible persistent consequences include:

- Abandoned infrastructure
- Ruins
- Former settlement sites
- Historical claims
- Cultural remnants
- Resource knowledge
- Event-specific modifiers

An abandoned Frontier should therefore be **resettlable**, not necessarily reset to a completely pristine Unsettled state.

---

# 4. Frontier Projects

The **Frontier Project** is the central mechanical object.

It answers:

> **What is being established here?**

It does not determine the permanent government of the system.

A project contains:

- Project Type
- Founder
- Sponsor
- Progress
- Requirements
- Modifiers
- Infrastructure
- Events
- Completion result

## 4.1 Core Project Types

### Settlement

Establish a permanent civilian population.

Potential results:

- New settlement
- Holding development
- New local character
- Population growth
- Cultural/religious presence

### Trade

Establish a commercial presence.

Potential results:

- Trade infrastructure
- Economic development
- Commercial buildings
- Merchant population
- Future trade opportunities

### Mining

Exploit valuable resources.

Potential results:

- Mining infrastructure
- Economic output
- Resource discovery
- Industrial development

### Military

Establish a defensive or strategic presence.

Potential results:

- Fortress
- Military installation
- Defensive infrastructure
- Strategic military character

### Religious

Establish a religious presence.

Potential results:

- Religious community
- Religious infrastructure
- Mission
- Religious character or institution

### Research

Investigate scientific, technological, archaeological, or anomalous opportunities.

Potential results:

- Research facility
- Discovery
- Special technology/resource
- Knowledge-related events

### Administrative

Prepare a system for closer political integration.

Potential results:

- Administrative infrastructure
- Increased control/development
- New official or governor-like character where supported by existing EotG/CK3 systems
- Easier future integration

Additional project types can be added later without changing the Frontier core.

---

# 5. Founder

Every Frontier Project has a **Founder** where appropriate.

The Founder is the character or entity primarily responsible for initiating the project.

The Founder may be:

- A landed ruler
- An adventurer
- A dynasty member
- A military leader
- A religious figure
- A merchant
- An organizational representative
- Another eligible character

The Founder does not automatically become the permanent ruler.

This distinction is important because the project may later produce a normal CK3 political structure.

---

# 6. Sponsor

A Frontier Project may have a **Sponsor**.

The Sponsor is the character, realm, organization, or other eligible entity providing resources or support.

The base Frontier system does not care *why* the Sponsor is providing that support.

A Sponsor may provide:

- Gold
- Population/settlers
- Development
- Infrastructure
- Protection
- Project acceleration
- Other appropriate resources

The Sponsor concept is deliberately generic.

### Important

A Sponsor is not automatically:

- A patron
- A liege
- A claimant
- An owner
- A political controller

Those relationships are determined by normal CK3 systems or later integration systems.

This is what allows future mechanics to use Frontiers without rewriting them.

---

# 7. Founder, Sponsor, and Political Owner

These should be separate concepts.

Example:

> **Founder:** Lady Veyra
> **Sponsor:** New Cauldron
> **Local Ruler:** House Veyra
> **Political Owner:** Existing county ruler

Later, another system may change the Sponsor without changing the Founder or political owner.

This creates useful emergent relationships without requiring Frontier Systems to create a custom political hierarchy.

---

# 8. Frontier Development

Frontier development represents how established the project has become.

It should **not** be implemented as a large standalone minigame or a prominent 0–100 player-facing bar unless implementation testing demonstrates a strong reason to do so.

Instead, progression should be driven primarily through existing CK3 values and project-specific state.

Potential inputs:

- Development
- Control
- Gold investment
- Infrastructure
- Buildings
- Population
- Characters
- Time
- Events
- Local conditions
- Resource discoveries
- Sponsor support

A project completes when its configured requirements are satisfied.

---

# 9. Frontier Infrastructure

Infrastructure represents what has physically been built during the Frontier phase.

Examples:

- Colonial Outpost
- Orbital Station
- Frontier Fortress
- Trade Station
- Mining Complex
- Research Facility
- Religious Mission
- Military Base
- Navigation Beacon

Infrastructure should support the Frontier Project rather than become a second political system.

Not every Frontier needs special infrastructure.

Some Frontiers should simply represent:

> A growing population with basic settlement infrastructure.

Others may develop into major strategic locations.

---

# 10. Frontier Traits

A Frontier may possess special traits describing the system.

Examples:

- Rich Resources
- Ancient Ruins
- Strategic Location
- Hostile Environment
- Habitable
- Remote
- Anomalous
- Dangerous
- Valuable Trade Route
- Ancient Infrastructure

Traits can affect:

- Project requirements
- Project speed
- Events
- Infrastructure
- Development
- Risks
- Potential rewards

Traits should remain lightweight and reusable.

---

# 11. Frontier Events

Events provide much of the narrative character of the system.

Possible events include:

### Positive

- Resource discovery
- Unexpected population growth
- Successful expedition
- Valuable ruins discovered
- New trade route
- Skilled settler arrives
- Infrastructure breakthrough

### Negative

- Supply failure
- Disease
- Hostile inhabitants
- Infrastructure collapse
- Resource shortage
- Internal dispute
- Raider attack
- Project abandonment

### Political

- Rival sponsor arrives
- Founder disputes ownership
- Local ruler demands concessions
- Religious dispute
- Cultural tension
- Sponsor requests greater influence

Events should preferably use existing CK3 mechanics rather than creating separate systems.

---

# 12. Failure and Abandonment

A Frontier Project may fail.

Failure should be contextual rather than simply a random "game over" roll.

Possible causes:

- Insufficient investment
- Low development
- Hostile environment
- War
- Loss of sponsor
- Founder death
- Political instability
- Repeated negative events
- Loss of local control

A failed project may:

- Become Abandoned
- Lose infrastructure
- Transfer sponsorship
- Change project type
- Continue under a new founder

The exact result should depend on circumstances.

---

# 13. Exploration and Discovery

Exploration is related to Frontier Systems but should not be required to build the entire feature.

A system can be:

- Known but Unsettled
- Unknown
- Partially explored
- Fully explored

The Frontier system should provide hooks for a future dedicated Exploration system.

For the initial implementation, a simple decision/event can be sufficient to reveal or enable an Unsettled system.

---

# 14. Integration Hooks

The system should expose generic hooks for future EotG systems.

Conceptual hooks include:

- `frontier_project_started`
- `frontier_project_progressed`
- `frontier_project_completed`
- `frontier_project_failed`
- `frontier_abandoned`
- `frontier_sponsor_changed`
- `frontier_development_changed`
- `frontier_settled`

Future systems can respond to these events without modifying the Frontier core.

---

# 15. Future System Integration

Frontier Systems should support future integrations such as:

## Corporations

A corporation can sponsor a Mining or Trade Frontier.

## Exploration

An exploration system can discover Unsettled systems and unlock Frontier Projects.

## Religion

A religious organization can establish Religious Frontiers.

## Mercenary Organizations

A mercenary group can establish Military Frontiers.

## Trade Networks

A trade system can accelerate Trade Frontiers.

## Greater Drifts

Greater Drift patronage can sponsor Frontiers and provide:

- Funding
- Settlers
- Infrastructure
- Protection
- Cultural influence
- Religious influence
- Project acceleration

The Frontier system itself does not need to know what a Greater Drift is.

---

# 16. Greater Drift Integration Principle

Greater Drifts are an **optional integration layer**.

The relationship should be:

> **Greater Drift → Frontier System**

not:

> **Frontier System → Greater Drift**

A Greater Drift may use Frontier Systems for:

- Sponsored settlement
- Rival settlement
- Foreign demographic influence
- Post-struggle development
- Settlement during the ten-year boom
- Long-term regional development

But Frontier Systems must work identically when no Greater Drift exists.

---

# 17. Greater Drift Patronage Integration

The Greater Drift design includes patronage relationships that can provide settlers, economic assistance, military support, political support, and stabilization assistance. The Puppet ↔ Partnership relationship determines how much assistance and dependence exists.

When integrated, a Greater Drift patron can simply become a Frontier **Sponsor**.

For example:

> Greater Drift Patron
> ↓
> Sponsors Frontier
> ↓
> Provides settlers and funding
> ↓
> Frontier develops
> ↓
> System becomes Settled

The Frontier does not need a special "Greater Drift Frontier" state.

---

# 18. Post-Struggle Boom Integration

The Greater Drift design calls for approximately ten years of post-struggle economic and developmental boom, including foreign funding, settlement, infrastructure, trade, and development.

Frontier Systems should be one possible physical manifestation of that boom.

The Greater Drift can increase:

- Frontier Project creation
- Frontier investment
- Settlement speed
- Foreign settlement
- Infrastructure construction

This should be implemented by modifying existing Frontier inputs rather than creating a second settlement system.

---

# 19. Cultural and Religious Integration

The Greater Drift design explicitly intends foreign settlement to create demographic, cultural, and religious effects over time.

Frontier Systems can provide the **settlement origin**.

The normal CK3 culture/religion systems should handle the resulting population and political consequences wherever practical.

Example:

> Foreign sponsor funds Frontier
> ↓
> Settlers arrive
> ↓
> Frontier develops
> ↓
> Settled system
> ↓
> Foreign culture/religion remains present
> ↓
> Existing CK3 systems determine longer-term demographic change

This prevents Frontier Systems from becoming a second culture-conversion system.

---

# 20. What Frontier Systems Does Not Own

The following should remain outside the core Frontier system:

- Government mechanics
- Adventurer progression
- Holy Order mechanics
- Claims
- Factions
- Wars
- Alliances
- Succession
- Culture conversion
- Religious conversion
- Permanent title hierarchy
- Greater Drift patronage
- Greater Drift stabilization
- Greater Drift degeneration

Frontier Systems can influence or trigger these systems, but should not recreate them.

---

# 21. Implementation Architecture

The system should be divided into modular components.

Recommended conceptual components:

1. Frontier state detection
2. Unsettled system definition
3. Frontier Project framework
4. Project eligibility
5. Founder assignment
6. Sponsor assignment
7. Project progression
8. Frontier infrastructure
9. Frontier traits
10. Frontier events
11. Project completion
12. Project failure
13. Abandonment
14. Settlement transition
15. Integration hooks

Each project type should be configurable rather than requiring a completely separate implementation.

# 23. Intended Player Experience

The player should see an Unsettled system and think:

> **"There is an opportunity here."**

They establish a Frontier.

Then:

> **"This might actually become something."**

They invest in it.

Unexpected events occur.

A rival may become interested.

The settlement grows.

Eventually:

> **"This is no longer a Frontier. This is part of our civilization."**

That should be the entire emotional arc.

The system should make the galaxy feel like it is **being built over time**, rather than presenting every star system as an already-complete political unit.

---

# 24. Final Design Summary

**Frontier Systems are a standalone, modular development framework for turning Unsettled star systems into established settlements.**

The core system consists of:

> **Unsettled → Frontier Project → Settled**

with:

- Founder
- Sponsor
- Project Type
- Development
- Infrastructure
- Traits
- Events
- Failure/Abandonment

The system deliberately does not create a parallel government, claims, factions, adventurer progression, culture system, or diplomatic system.

Its job is simple:

> **Provide a believable intermediate stage between an untouched star system and a normal CK3 political/economic system.**

Future systems—including the Greater Drift—can then use Frontiers as a modular building block.

---

# 25. Design Requirement

> **Frontier Systems must be fully playable before Greater Drifts are implemented. Greater Drift integration must be additive and must not be required for the Frontier feature to function.**
